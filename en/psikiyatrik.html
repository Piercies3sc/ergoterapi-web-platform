Psychiatric Occupational Therapy

Aims for individuals experiencing mental health challenges or psychiatric illnesses to actively participate in social life, production, and daily routines again.

Healing Through Meaningful Activities

Mental health problems can lead to social isolation by weakening an individual's motivation, energy levels, time structuring skills, and social communication. Psychiatric occupational therapy (Community Mental Health) utilizes the power of "meaningful activities and occupations" in addition to medical treatment during the recovery process.

By working closely with the individual, our main goal is to establish healthy routines throughout the day, develop stress-coping skills, and ensure the person becomes a productive individual again (working, studying, or having hobbies).

Core Intervention Areas

Establishing Routines

Establishing a healthy daily cycle by organizing sleep, nutrition, and leisure activities.

Social Skills Training

Supporting skills in communication, self-expression, setting boundaries, and existing in society.

Stress and Anger Management

Providing emotion regulation and developing strategies for coping with challenging situations.

Vocational Rehabilitation

Providing necessary adaptations and increasing motivation during the return-to-work or school process.

Therapy Process

Our person-centered, recovery-focused approach.

1

Psychosocial Assessment

Analysis of the person's interests, roles, and daily life difficulties.

2

Individualized Goals

Setting realistic and meaningful goals that will motivate the person.

3

Activity-Based Intervention

Developing skills and self-confidence through selected meaningful activities.

4

Community Reintegration

Transferring acquired skills to daily life, family, and work environments.
